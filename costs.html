Section 6 · Costs (US)

What treatment costs — and how to get your real number

Products on this site carry prices read from official manufacturer pages (see Products & Prices). Medical care is different: there is no official public price list for a shoulder exam, a physical-therapy course, an injection or surgery — the same service has dozens of prices depending on your insurance, your ZIP code and whether the facility is an office, an outpatient department or a surgery center. So this page does not invent dollar figures. It gives you the official tools that produce your number, and it names what each pathway involves.

Every link on this page was opened and read on September 20, 2026 (fourth verification pass). Source IDs (S49–S54) match Sources.

⚠ Read this first This page is general information about US health-care pricing, not insurance, billing or medical advice. Coverage rules depend on your plan, your state and your employer. Confirm anything that matters with your insurer or plan administrator before committing to treatment or a purchase.

2. What insurance actually covers (the structure, not the price)

  • Marketplace / ACA plans (individual and small-group): “Rehabilitative and habilitative services and devices (services and devices to help people with injuries, disabilities, or chronic conditions gain or recover mental and physical skills)” is one of the 10 essential health benefits that Marketplace plans must cover — physical therapy sits in that category. Cost-sharing (deductible, copay, coinsurance) still applies to most covered services S54. Two important carve-outs on the same official page: large employers that self-insure are not required to provide the essential health benefits, and grandfathered plans may not — so “check with your employer or plan” is the honest answer if either applies to you S54.
  • Medicare (65+, or younger with a qualifying disability or ESRD): Part B helps pay for medically necessary outpatient physical therapy with no annual limit, and after the Part B deductible you pay 20% of the Medicare-approved amount S51. For 2026 the official figures are a $283 Part B deductible and a $202.90 standard monthly premium S52. If you are 36–40 and not on Medicare, treat this as a benchmark and a coverage model, not as your situation.
  • Anything with a high deductible or no coverage: the federal No Surprises Act gives you a specific right — usually, if you don’t have or don’t use health insurance, providers must give you a good faith estimate of what your care will cost when you schedule services at least 3 business days in advance or when you ask for one. If the final bill is at least $400 more than the estimate, you may be able to dispute it S49.
HSA / FSA The Wife Pillow vendor page states HSA/FSA eligibility with a letter of medical necessity — that is the vendor’s claim, and it is the only HSA/FSA statement left on the Products page (a Branch checkout claim could not be re-verified in pass 4 and was removed). Eligibility is decided by your plan administrator and reimbursement is not guaranteed by the vendor: confirm with your plan before buying on that basis.

5. The five-question price script (bring it to the appointment)

  1. “What are the billing codes for this?” Ask for the CPT/HCPCS codes for the visit, the injection, the imaging and each therapy session. The Medicare lookup searches “by procedure name or code”, and insurers price by code S50.
  2. “Where will this be done — your office, a hospital outpatient department, or a surgery center?” Facility type is a first-order cost driver in the official lookup S50.
  3. “Can you give me a good faith estimate in writing?” If you’re uninsured or not using insurance, providers usually must provide one when you ask or schedule ≥3 business days ahead — and there is a $400 discrepancy threshold you can dispute S49.
  4. “What does my plan say?” Call the number on your card with the codes and ask for your allowed amount, copay/coinsurance and any prior-authorization requirement. If your plan is Marketplace-based, note that rehabilitative services are in the required benefit set but cost-sharing applies — and that self-insured employer plans and grandfathered plans work differently S54.
  5. “Is there a cheaper equivalent setting?” For outpatient procedures, compare the ambulatory-surgical-center column against the hospital-outpatient column in the official lookup before you book S50; for therapy, compare a standalone clinic with a hospital-run department.
Do this first, every time Run the escalation timeline before spending anything: if it has been less than 2–4 weeks of consistent self-care and exercise with no red flags, the free rung is the correct next step — MedlinePlus says to contact a provider if pain lasts more than 2–4 weeks despite home treatment S1.
