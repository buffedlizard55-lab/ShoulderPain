SHOULDER & SHOULDER-BLADE PAINWhen to get help ↗

Desk & room

Less reaching. More moving.

A practical workstation and living-space plan for a small room.

A comfortable setup is adjustable — not a posture you freeze in.

OSHA recommends opportunities to vary work and avoid prolonged sitting or standing. Workstation changes address exposure; they do not diagnose or repair a shoulder injury. [W1] [W3]

Adjust the existing setup first

Illustrative only · not to scale

  1. Support your body. Back supported, feet flat or on a stable footrest, room under the desk for knees and legs. [W1]
  2. Bring work closer. Relax the shoulders, keep elbows close, and put the mouse beside the keyboard at the same level. [W1]
  3. Set the screen for your eyes. Directly in front; top at or slightly below eye level. Generally 20–40 inches from eyes to screen, with readable text. [W2]
  4. Change position and tasks. Use short breaks and alternate computer with non-computer tasks. [W3]

Small-room fit checks before buying

Project checklist: no room dimensions, body measurements or equipment inventory were supplied. The products are candidates, not confirmed fits.

In use

  • Desktop width and depth, plus chair travel and an unobstructed exit.
  • Seated elbow height and standing elbow height against desk limits.
  • Space for screen distance, keyboard and mouse without forward reaching.
  • Foot support, knee clearance, glare and outlets.

When stored

  • Full folded length, width and thickness — not just the top size.
  • Base/caster footprint and door clearance along the removal route.
  • Whether it needs lifting; a heavy folding desk is not necessarily easy to remove.
  • Space to put the monitor and other equipment before folding.

A shallow desk is not automatically unusable, and a deep desk is not automatically ergonomic. The OSHA 20–40 inch guideline is eye-to-screen distance, not a mandatory desktop depth. [W2]

A workable pack-away layout

Project design suggestions, not a medically validated room plan.

  1. One removable work surface. A flip-top desk can store by a wall, a wall-folding top stays attached to the wall, or a fully collapsing desk may fit under a bed. Compare actual dimensions and weight on the product page.
  2. A separate home for loose equipment. Use an existing shelf or container for the keyboard, mouse and cables. Keep frequently used items within comfortable reach during work. [W1] [M4]
  3. No assumed monitor-arm compatibility. Confirm top material, manufacturer approval, clamping area, stability, load and clearance through folding. Top thickness alone does not establish safety.
  4. Clear the folding path. Remove loose equipment as instructed, keep fingers and cables out of hinges, and use brakes/locks according to the product manual. These are conservative project precautions, not product-specific safety certification.
  5. Use the existing sleep setup first. Try the non-painful side or back with pillow support rather than buying a mattress on the assumption it will treat the shoulder. [M4]

For a long computer day

Suggested routine: use an existing timer to prompt a brief position change or walk every 30–60 minutes, as comfortable. Alternate tasks and avoid replacing a long sitting block with a long standing block. The timing is a project reminder, not an evidence-based prescription; the variation principle comes from OSHA. [W1] [W3]

Do not force shoulders “back and down” all day. Use supported, relaxed positions and movement opportunities rather than treating posture as a test of willpower. This is the project’s application of OSHA’s relaxed-shoulder and variability guidance. [W1]
