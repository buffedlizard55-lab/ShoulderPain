SHOULDER & SHOULDER-BLADE PAINWhen to get help ↗

Exercises

Move gently. Build gradually.

Source-based exercise examples to review with a clinician or physiotherapist.

These are examples to review, not “your rehab program.”

AAOS calls this a general conditioning program to perform under a doctor’s supervision, with exercises selected by a doctor or physical therapist. You should not feel pain during the exercises; stop and discuss pain with your clinician/PT. [E1] Visible winging, new weakness, an injury or neurologic symptoms should be assessed before loading the shoulder. [M3] Urgent symptoms override exercise →

Before you start

AAOS recommends 5–10 minutes of low-impact warm-up such as walking before its program. Its 4–6 week program duration is general guidance, not a reason to delay assessment or a guaranteed recovery period. [E1]

Project safety approach: do not force range, add weights or work through sharp pain to hit a repetition target. The dose below is the published example, not a required starting dose for you.

A / Gentle mobility examples

NO SPECIAL EQUIPMENT

1. Pendulum

  1. Lean forward, supporting one hand on a stable counter or table.
  2. Let the other arm hang freely.
  3. Gently swing forward/back, side-to-side and in circles; repeat the sequence with the other arm.

Do not round your back or lock your knees. [E1]

AAOS example: 2 sets of 10 · 5–6 days/week. [E1]

Project safety: do not lean on an unlocked folding top or rolling desk.

NO SPECIAL EQUIPMENT

2. Crossover arm stretch

  1. Relax your shoulders.
  2. Gently bring one arm across the chest, holding at the upper arm.
  3. Hold for 30 seconds, then relax for 30 seconds; repeat with the other arm.

Do not pull on or press the elbow. Stop if painful. [E1]

AAOS example: 4 repetitions each side · 5–6 days/week. [E1]

B / Strengthening after assessment

These are not first-line self-tests for a possible tear or nerve injury. Ask which, if any, match your clinical findings.

3. Standing row

With a comfortably resistant band securely anchored, keep the elbow bent near your side, slowly pull it straight back and return slowly. AAOS cues a shoulder-blade squeeze on the pull. [E1]

AAOS example: 3 sets of 8 · 3 days/week; progress to 3 sets of 12 as easier. [E1]

4. External rotation, elbow at side

With a securely anchored comfortable-resistance band and bent elbow at your side, slowly rotate the arm outward without moving the elbow away from the body; return slowly. [E1]

AAOS example: 3 sets of 8 · 3 days/week; progress to 3 sets of 12 as easier. [E1]

Project equipment rule: use a clinician-approved band/anchor setup and follow its instructions. Inspect for damage and keep the recoil path clear.
