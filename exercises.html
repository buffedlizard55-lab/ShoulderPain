Section 3 · Movement plan

Gentle shoulder conditioning and desk breaks

This is a conservative starter, not a diagnosis-specific winging prescription. It is designed for a stable, non-urgent pattern where movement is tolerable. A physical therapist should choose and progress the plan if there is visible winging, new weakness, numbness/tingling, a traumatic onset, or persistent pain.

Exercise instructions/doses checked against AAOS OrthoInfo and MedlinePlus on September 20, 2026. Doses marked “AAOS” belong to AAOS' general conditioning program, not to this person's diagnosis.

Stop and get assessed Do not exercise through sharp pain, sudden weakness, instability, numbness/tingling, or a visible wing that is new or worsening. AAOS says not to ignore pain during an exercise and recommends doctor/PT supervision; MedlinePlus says to check exercise technique and selection with a provider/PT. S44 S46

Rules before the routine

  1. Screen first: use the red flags page. Visible scapular winging or clear loss of strength is a reason for an examination, not a reason to add more bands.
  2. Warm up: AAOS recommends 5–10 minutes of low-impact activity such as walking or a stationary bicycle before its program. S44
  3. Pain rule: use a comfortable range; do not force an end-range stretch or “test” the injury. AAOS says there should be no pain during an exercise. S44
  4. Small load: use no weight or a light, comfortable band at first. MedlinePlus specifically notes that small rotator-cuff muscles may call for smaller weights and higher repetitions, with PT form checks. S46
  5. Progress one thing: add repetitions or resistance only when the movement is controlled and symptom-neutral during and after the session. This is a conservative project rule, not a promise about healing time.

A simple starter session

Choose two mobility options and one strengthening option at first. The complete AAOS program contains more exercises; this smaller set is easier to audit and less likely to become a high-volume “winging workout.”

Pendulum Support one hand on a counter, let the other arm hang, and gently swing forward/back, side-to-side, then in small circles. Do not round the back or lock the knees. 2 sets of 10; 5–6 days/week Gentle movement option. Stop if the shoulder feels unstable or the motion increases pain. S44
Crossover arm stretch Relax the shoulders; gently draw one arm across the chest by holding the upper arm, not the elbow. Feel a stretch at the back of the shoulder, not pain. 4 each side; hold 30 seconds; 5–6 days/week Mobility option. Skip or ask a PT if cross-body motion pinches or the injury is recent. S44
Scapula setting Lie on the stomach with a pillow under the forehead if needed. Gently draw the shoulder blades together and down, ease halfway out, and hold without tensing the neck. 10 repetitions; hold 10 seconds; 3 days/week Low-load awareness/activation. It is not a test for nerve function and should not be forced. S44
Standing row with a band Anchor the band securely, keep the elbows bent and close enough to avoid shrugging, pull the elbows back, squeeze the shoulder blades gently, and return slowly. 3 sets of 8; 3 days/week; AAOS says progress to 3 sets of 12 as easier Strength option for upper-back support. Use the manufacturer’s anchoring/snapback safety rules; do not anchor to an unstable door or point the band at the face. S44 S40
Standing external rotation With the elbow bent and close to the side, rotate the forearm outward against a light band while keeping the shoulder relaxed. Return slowly. 3 sets of 8; 3 days/week; AAOS says progress to 3 sets of 12 as easier Rotator-cuff strengthening option. Do not force range, abduct the arm, or increase resistance to compensate for poor control. S44 S46

AAOS describes its general conditioning program as 4–6 weeks, with 2–3 days/week for maintenance after recovery, under a doctor/PT’s supervision. That timeline is not a promise that this person's pain or winging will resolve in 4–6 weeks. S44

If the scapula visibly wings

Do not assume a band routine will fix it. AAOS describes scapular disorders from muscle imbalance/tightness, nerve or bone/joint injury and recommends examination-based care. The NCBI clinical review identifies long thoracic, spinal accessory, and dorsal scapular nerve patterns and discusses EMG/nerve-conduction testing. A visible wing, new drooping, or clear weakness deserves clinician/PT assessment before adding load. S27 S28

Irregularity flag “Wing pain” may mean pain beside the blade, a feeling of movement, or a visible wing. Those are not interchangeable findings. Record which one you mean in the pain log and show a clinician a photo/video of the movement only if it can be captured safely; do not delay care to create one.

A realistic 12-hour desk-break schedule

OSHA recommends changing posture, taking short rest breaks, moving, and alternating tasks, but it does not set one universal shoulder-break interval. The schedule below is a practical reminder system, not a medical dose. The American Optometric Association’s 20-20-20 rule is for digital eye strain; it should not be advertised as a shoulder treatment. S16 S30

How to decide whether to continue

  • Continue gently: movement is controlled, pain is absent or clearly tolerable, and symptoms are not worse later that day or the following morning.
  • Reduce volume/load: soreness or ache lingers, form changes, or the next morning is clearly worse. Return to mobility only or pause and ask a PT.
  • Stop and seek care: sharp pain, sudden weakness, numbness/tingling, instability, new visible winging, trauma, or progressive symptoms. S1 S7 S28
