If you have sudden left shoulder pain with chest pressure, shortness of breath, jaw/neck pain, sweating, or dizziness, call 911 immediately — that pattern can be a heart attack (MedlinePlus S1). If you can't move your arm, or the shoulder looks deformed after an injury, seek urgent care (Mayo Clinic S7).

The situation in one paragraph

I'm a healthy male, roughly 36–40, with pain in my left shoulder and a persistent “wing” feeling around the shoulder blade (scapula). Three likely contributors based on my lifestyle: (1) I sleep on my left side, (2) I work out and may have strained or injured the muscle group, and (3) I sit at a desk 12+ hours a day, sometimes slouched or frozen in one position. This project gathers the best-verified information on what could be causing this, what to do about it medically, a daily exercise plan, and a desk/furniture plan that fits a small room and can be folded away quickly.

Bottom line (verified takeaways)

  • Shoulder pain has multiple possible causes, including rotator-cuff problems, bursitis, muscle strain, nerve injury, and poor shoulder posture/mechanics; a list of possibilities is not a diagnosis S1.
  • Long static sitting at a computer loads the neck and shoulder muscles that hold your head up; OSHA recommends breaking up static postures with micro-breaks and posture changes S16.
  • “Winging” is a description rather than a diagnosis: AAOS and NCBI discuss muscle imbalance, nerve dysfunction, and other causes, so a clinician should confirm the pattern—especially with visible protrusion or weakness S28 S27.
  • Physical therapy and diagnosis-appropriate exercise are common conservative options. An honest read of the evidence: a 2016 Cochrane review (searches through March 2015) found pain outcomes “little or no different” from placebo in its main comparison (with a small function gain); a later synthesis reported high-quality evidence in one specific-versus-nonspecific exercise comparison; and a 2023 randomized trial found improvement in supervised subacromial-pain programs — useful support for progressive care, not a universal winging prescription S22 S24 S23.
  • Sleep: the official self-care line is “lie either on the side that is not in pain or on your back” — and impingement-type shoulder pain is specifically aggravated by “lying on the involved side” S2 S45.
  • Desk: arms too high or too low, armrests set too high, and a mouse placed too far away are each named shoulder-pain causes in state OSHA guidance — the cheapest fixes on the whole site S21.
  • There are genuine foldable, small-space desk options from about $25.50 to $887 in the checked snapshots; prices/stock change, so the manufacturer links must be re-opened before buying S33 S34 S32.

Verification & honesty policy

Every medical claim on this site carries a source chip (e.g., S1) that links to the Sources page, where each source lists its publisher, trust tier, URL, and the date it was checked. Product prices are dated snapshots; manufacturer pages are primary where available, while retailer/distributor prices are badged and still need re-checking before purchase. Where the evidence is weak or mixed (e.g., how much better therapy is versus placebo), the site says so instead of sugar-coating it. When a pass finds an error in the site's own earlier text, the correction is logged rather than made silently (pass 2 and pass 3). Items flagged for human review live on the Next Steps page.
