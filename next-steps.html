What to do next, what is uncertain, and what changed

This page keeps the remaining work and irregularities visible instead of filling gaps with assumptions. The site can organize evidence and decisions; it cannot examine a shoulder, verify a visible wing, or guarantee a product fit.

Status reviewed September 20, 2026 after the implementation, bug/assumption, and final accuracy/completeness passes. See Pass 1, Pass 2, and Pass 3.

Recommended next steps for the person with symptoms

  1. Screen for red flags now. Emergency chest symptoms, sudden severe pain, deformity, sudden swelling, inability to move/use the arm, or concerning neurologic symptoms override every home plan. Read the red flags. S1 S7
  2. Clarify “wing.” Is it pain beside the shoulder blade, a sensation of movement, or a blade visibly lifting away from the ribs? Record the difference; new visible winging or weakness deserves clinical assessment. S27 S28
  3. Run a low-risk 1–2 week experiment only if stable. Reduce aggravating gym loads, keep tolerable motion, sleep off the sore side, vary the desk position, and use the pain-free exercise rules. Log morning/evening pain, sleep side, desk hours, breaks, and weakness/tingling in the offline tracker. S2 S16
  4. Escalate instead of extending the experiment indefinitely. MedlinePlus gives a 2–4 week home-treatment threshold; Mayo lists worsening pain or harder movement as reasons to seek care. Bring the exported log to a primary-care clinician, orthopedist, or physical therapist. S1 S7

Project limitations

  • No examination or diagnosis: public guidance cannot determine whether pain is rotator-cuff, bursal, cervical, nerve-related, referred, or another condition.
  • Evidence is condition-specific: a general AAOS conditioning dose or a small RCT is not a custom prescription. The Cochrane review used here searched through March 2015 and should not be presented as current definitive proof. S22
  • Posture is not a complete explanation: OSHA/NIOSH guidance supports changing exposure and reducing awkward/static loading; it does not prove that slouching caused a visible wing.
  • Prices are temporary: the site is static GitHub Pages and does not monitor price/stock. All listed product prices are USD snapshots from US pages, excluding possible tax/shipping unless the row says otherwise.
  • Privacy is device-level: the Pain Log makes no network request and uses localStorage, but anyone with access to the browser/device can potentially access the entries. Exported JSON/CSV files are sensitive and should be stored carefully.
  • Regional/access limitations: sources, products, currency, and healthcare escalation language are primarily US-oriented. Product availability and clinical pathways differ elsewhere.
  • No guarantee: following this site cannot guarantee symptom improvement, prevent injury, or substitute for emergency or professional care.
