🚧 Limitations that block a “perfect” version

  • No personal data: I can't examine the shoulder, see the winging, or know the room's geometry. Everything is therefore structured as decision rules (“if X, then Y”) rather than a single prescription.
  • No clinical authority: this is a research compilation. The strongest claim the evidence supports is “start self-care + exercise, checkpoint at 2 weeks, escalate to a clinician if it fails” — and that's exactly the structure used.
  • Price volatility: furniture and accessory prices change weekly (and, as pass 2 showed, within a day); a static site goes stale. Re-verify before any purchase is made from this page.
  • Small-study evidence base: several cited trials are small (e.g., n=33); the Cochrane base for rotator-cuff therapy is thin. The site states this rather than hiding it, but it means “evidence-based” here is stronger than it looks.
  • Single-region assumption: US sources, USD prices, US retail. If any factor differs, the product section needs a regional pass.
  • No live price monitoring: a static GitHub Pages site can't track prices; that would need a workflow (e.g., a scheduled job that re-fetches the 12 product pages and updates a JSON price cache) — suggested for a later session.
  • Product-fit claims are read, not tested: nothing on the Products page has been physically tried; specs are quoted from manufacturer pages. Where a fit question is open (monitor arm on the COMHOMA top; MARKUS armrest height) it is flagged rather than assumed.
