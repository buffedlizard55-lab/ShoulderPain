Section 6 · Tracking tool

Daily pain log

Everything on this site ends in two checkpoints: “if it isn't better after 2–4 weeks of self-care, book a clinician” and “re-evaluate the exercise plan at 6 weeks.” This page makes those decisions measurable — log once in the morning and once at night (or once a day if that's more realistic).

🔒 Privacy: everything stays on this device This page has no backend, no analytics, and no network calls. Entries are stored in your browser's localStorage under the key shoulderpain-log-v1 and never leave your computer. Clearing your browser data deletes them — use Export below to keep a backup file. A pain log is also useful to show a clinician at the visits described on the Treatment page. Do not use this page to decide whether an emergency symptom is safe to wait on.
Irregularity flags are deliberately explicit Record a visible wing, new weakness/dropping objects, numbness/tingling, or a sudden injury/onset when present. A checked flag is a prompt to read Red flags and consider clinical assessment; it is not a diagnosis. The tracker does not silently “correct” a flagged entry.
Not medical advice The checkpoint messages below are reminders based on the escalation plan on the Treatment page (MedlinePlus: contact a provider if pain persists more than 2–4 weeks despite home treatment S1; Mayo: worsening pain or harder movement warrants a visit S7) — they are not a diagnosis. Red flags always override the calendar: see the Causes page.
