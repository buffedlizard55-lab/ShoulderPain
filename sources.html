Sources & verification log

This is the site's evidence map. Each source has a direct link, publisher/tier, and a narrow description of what it is used to support. Medical sources are preferred for medical claims; manufacturer pages are used for product specifications/prices; lower-tier or commercial pages are explicitly labelled.

Three explicit review passes completed September 20, 2026: implementation/claim coverage, bug + missing-requirement + assumption review, then final accuracy/completeness/code-quality re-check. Product prices and stock are snapshots and can change immediately.

Claim ledger: what the site says, and why

“Source-linked” does not mean “diagnosed.” The wording below is the boundary used when reviewing every user-facing medical recommendation.

Load-bearing claims reviewed line by line
Claim / recommendationEvidence usedBoundary kept in the site
Shoulder-area pain has multiple possible causes and can be referred from elsewhere; chest symptoms and major loss of function can be urgent.S1, S7, S10Causes pages use “possible,” not a remote diagnosis; emergency language is limited to the cited warning patterns.
Long static computer postures can fatigue neck/shoulder muscles; changing posture, taking short breaks, moving, and alternating tasks are reasonable work-process controls.S15, S16, S20OSHA does not set one universal shoulder-break minute count. The example schedule is labelled a project suggestion.
There is no single perfect workstation posture; monitor, keyboard/mouse, elbow, feet, and shoulder targets are practical setup goals.S15, S17, S21Federal OSHA’s “at least 20 inches” monitor distance is not confused with Oregon OSHA’s approximately 16–29 inches or 24-inch work-surface depth.
Side sleeping on an irritable shoulder can aggravate symptoms; sleeping on the pain-free side or back and keeping the neck aligned are low-cost experiments.S2, S13, S45Sleep position is described as an aggravator/experiment, not proof of the underlying cause.
Scapular dyskinesis/winging can involve muscle imbalance, nerve injury, or bone/joint problems; wall-push observation, strength examination, and sometimes EMG/nerve studies may be relevant.S27, S28Visible winging or new weakness is a reason for assessment, not a reason to self-prescribe a nerve-specific program.
MedlinePlus rotator-cuff self-care gives specific ice/heat and activity-modification language.S2The 20-minute ice timing is attributed to that page; no universal timing is invented for every diagnosis.
OTC pain medicines have benefits and serious contraindications/interactions; diclofenac topical is not presented as a blanket shoulder treatment.S3, S4, S5, S6, S8No dose is given. The reader is directed to the current label/pharmacist/clinician.
AAOS gives a general conditioning program with pain-free rules and exercise-specific doses; MedlinePlus advises PT/provider input.S44, S46AAOS doses are labelled general conditioning guidance, not a diagnosis-specific winging prescription.
Evidence for exercise is useful but mixed and condition-specific; the Cochrane review searched studies through March 2015.S22, S23, S24No “exercise always beats everything” or current-definitive-evidence claim is made.
Injection, imaging, specialist care, and surgery depend on the confirmed problem and failure/appropriateness of conservative care.S9, S10, S25, S28These are escalation options, not recommendations for the user without examination.
Products are evaluated against space, foldability, adjustability, and source-page specifications.S32–S48No product is claimed to treat or cure shoulder pain; price, stock, fit, warranty, and shipping are dynamic.

How to manually review a claim

  1. Click the source chip beside the sentence, then open the direct link in the row above.
  2. Check the publisher, date/update status, population/condition, and whether the page says “may,” “can,” or gives a diagnosis-specific recommendation.
  3. For a product, check the live variant, currency, shipping/tax, stock, returns, measurements, and whether your desk/monitor/chair actually matches the specification.
  4. For symptoms or medication, use the site as a question list for a clinician/pharmacist—not as permission to delay urgent care.
